Introduction

Salmonella is a major foodborne pathogen responsible for 1.35 million gastroenteritis every year. Salmonella is present along the continuum of several food chains, like poultry and produce, and it could harbor plasmids along the production chain. Acquiring such plasmids play a critical role in antimicrobial resistance (AMR), virulence, stress tolerance, and host adaptation. Many epidemiologically important resistance genes are plasmid borne, such as extended-spectrum β-lactamases, colistin resistance, and multidrug efflux systems. Understanding the incidence of plasmids in Salmonella is essential for food safety surveillance, outbreak investigations, and risk assessment.

General Background Information

Advances in whole-genome sequencing (WGS) have enabled large-scale surveillance of bacterial pathogens. The National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) has public repositories such as Pathogen Detection system, which integrate thousands of foodborne pathogens genomes, such as Salmonella, which are generated through routine surveillance and research efforts. These resources provide a powerful framework to study plasmid prevalence and diversity across lineages, sources, and periods of time. The objective of this study is to characterize plasmid incidence in Salmonella using publicly available WGS data. This could provide insights into how frequently plasmids are detected and how their presence may vary across sources. e.g. clinical, enviroment, food, etc.

Description of data and data source

The data used in this analysis will be obtained from two publicly available NCBI resources:

NCBI Pathogen Detection (PD) The database includes assembled genomes, isolation source, collection date, and geographic information.

NCBI Datasets – Genomes The database includes assembled genomes, genome annotations, assembly statistics, and annotation files that can be used to identify plasmid-associated sequences or plasmid replicon types.

Together, these datasets contain genomic and metadata information necessary to assess the presence or absence of plasmids across a large collection of Salmonella isolates.

Questions/Hypotheses to be addressed

This analysis will address the following research questions:

Does plasmid incidence vary across different Salmonella serovars?

Are there observable patterns in plasmid presence associated with isolate with other factors, such as source (e.g., human, poultry, environmental) or collection year?

Based on existing literature, the hypothesis is that higher plasmid incidence is expected in isolates associated with food-animal production and clinical sources due to selective pressures such as antimicrobial use.
